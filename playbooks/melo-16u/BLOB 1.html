BLOB · Play 1

Baseline Out of Bounds · Half Court · Melo 16U

Stack Action

Phase 1 — Stack Release

1
The stack is a loaded spring. 5, 2, 1, and 4 are stacked tight on the right side of the key. Nobody moves until 3 triggers the play. The tighter the stack, the harder it is to guard — the defense has to pick someone to overplay, and they can't cover all four.
2
5 goes through the paint — not around it. 5 is at the bottom of the stack. The moment the play starts, explode across the lane to the weak-side rim. Cut through the paint. The defender has to fight through traffic to follow. That's the whole point.
3
2 peels to the right corner simultaneously. 2 breaks at the same moment as 5 — same breath, same trigger. Sprint to the ball-side corner at the 3-point line. This opens the corner as a second read for 3 and pulls a defender away from the paint.
4
3 reads and delivers fast. 3 has two reads in Phase 1: 5 at the weak-side rim (primary), or 2 in the right corner (secondary). The window on 5 is short. If it's there, take it. If not, kick to 2.
For Players Teaching cue: "5 and 2 — you go on the same breath. Not one, then the other. Same breath."

When the stack breaks in two directions at once, the defense has to split. That's the advantage. The play creates it in the first second — if you hesitate, you give it back.
Coach's Eye Watch how the baseline defender reacts on the break. If they chase 5 across the paint, the corner opens for 2. If they hold for the corner, the lob to 5 at the rim is there. 3 makes one read — that's the whole decision tree.
Structural Bridge → Phase 2 While 5 and 2 are clearing, the defense's eyes follow the movement — that's the misdirection. In that same split second, 1 turns back into the stack and hunts 4's defender. Phase 1 creates the distraction. Phase 2 is the attack hidden inside it.

Phase 2 — Screen & Outlet

1
1 finds the body. After the stack releases, 1 turns and locates 4's defender. Plant a legal back screen — a T-action — directly in the defender's path. Make contact. This is not a soft pick. Hold it long enough for 4 to read and cut.
2
4 reads the screen and attacks the rim. 4 watches the defender: goes under the screen → dive straight to the basket. Fights over the screen → curl and flatten toward the rim. The rule never changes: go toward the basket first. Never fade away.
3
1 is the outlet — sprint to the wing. The moment the screen is set, 1 releases and sprints hard to the ball-side wing. If 4's lane is taken, 3 fires to 1 at the wing. Catch in rhythm. Be a threat.
4
3 delivers to the open man. 3 now has three live reads: 4 diving to the rim (primary), 1 at the ball-side wing (outlet), or 2 still in the corner. Read the defense — not the script.
For Players Teaching cue: "1 — find the body, then go get the ball."

The most common mistake: 1 leaves the screen early to get open, so the screen never actually sets and the defense recovers. Hold it one beat longer than feels comfortable — then sprint to the wing.
Coach's Eye The danger for the defense is overloading on Phase 1 — chasing 5 and 2. When that happens, 4's defender is navigating a back screen with no help in the paint. That's the layup this play is designed to create.

If the defense switches to handle the screen, 1 comes off with a size mismatch going to the wing. Either answer is a win for the offense.
Play Summary Play 1 is a two-phase misdirection BLOB. Phase 1 stretches the defense horizontally — rim and corner — to open the paint. Phase 2 attacks the vacated middle with a back screen and a basket cut. When both phases are run with discipline and timing, the defense is always wrong: guard the rim cut or guard the Phase 1 breaks — never both at once.
